# Discovery Call Script

For 30-min Zoom intro calls with mid-market commercial GCs. **Goal: listen, not pitch.** A construction prospect is far more likely to commit to a follow-up if they feel heard than if they feel sold.

---

## Pre-call (5 min before)

- Confirm who's on the call (Principal? Controller? PM? More than one?)
- Pull up their company website. Note: any project they're proud of, any indication of size, any tech mentioned.
- Have your one-pager open in another tab. Have 3–4 mockups ready: Dashboard → CO Approval Trail → AIA Pay App → Drift Detail. **Do not show until they ask** or you've heard their pain (~minute 15+).

## Open (60 seconds)

> "Thanks for the time. Quick context: I'm building software for SMB commercial GCs. I've talked to a few firms about their monthly pay-app cycle and there's a clear pattern of pain. Today I mostly want to learn how you run yours. I'll show you what we've built only if it's relevant — and if you're up for showing me an Excel sheet or two of yours, even better. Sound good?"

Tone: peer-to-peer, not salesperson-to-prospect. You're a fellow operator solving a problem.

## Questions, in priority order

### Block A — their workflow (10–15 min)

1. **"Walk me through your last monthly pay-app cycle. From the day a sub submits to you, to the day the owner pays you."** — Listen for: tools mentioned, who does what, where it stalls.
2. **"How many tools touched that cycle?"** — Probe: "Excel? QuickBooks? Cantina? Any others?"
3. **"What was the biggest delay or friction?"** — Best question on the call. Their answer is your wedge.
4. **"How many active projects are you managing right now? How many subs across all projects?"** — Sizing question.
5. **"Who actually generates your AIA G702/G703 — and what tool are they using?"**

### Block B — the gap-stop (5–10 min)

6. **"When a sub submits a pay app that exceeds their contract amount — say, because a change order was approved by the owner but hasn't been entered into your tracker yet — what happens?"** — This is the question. If they laugh and say "constantly", validation locked.
7. **"How often does that happen? Once a month? Per project?"**
8. **"Who chases it down when it does?"**

### Block C — qualification (5 min)

9. **"What's your annual construction volume?"** — Confirms they're in ICP.
10. **"Are you the person who'd decide on something like this, or is there a partner / CFO / IT person involved?"** — Always ask. Prevents wasted follow-ups.

### If signals are strong, transition (~minute 15–20):

11. **"What I just heard you describe — that's exactly what we built. Can I show you a few screens?"**

Walk them through, in this order:
- Dashboard (drift alerts visible)
- Change Order with Approval Trail (focus on the green propagation banner)
- AIA Pay App preview (the $289,177.06 cobalt focal)
- Drift Detail (the wedge)

Keep talking minimal. Let them ask questions. Their questions = their pain points = your roadmap.

### Close (5 min)

12. **"What questions do you have?"** — Always.
13. **"If we built this for you, would you actually use it? What would have to be true?"** — Listen for objections; capture for the cheat sheet.
14. **"Can I send you the one-pager and a few screenshots to share internally?"** — Permission-based follow-up.
15. **"Would you be open to a 15-min follow-up after you've shared it?"** — Book it on the call if possible.

## After-call (10 min)

- Write 3 sentences in a CRM (Notion, Airtable, even Google Sheets):
  - **Pain points heard** (their words, not yours)
  - **Sizing** (revenue, projects, subs)
  - **Decision-maker status** (yes/no, who else)
  - **Next step + date**
- Update objection cheat sheet if they raised something new.
- Send the one-pager + mockup PDFs within 4 hours of the call. Construction folks remember responsiveness.

## Things NOT to do on the call

- Don't open with "let me show you what we've built" — pitch first kills conversation.
- Don't hide pricing if they ask. Direct: "$799/mo flat per firm." Confidence is contagious; haggling on the first call signals you don't believe in your price.
- Don't promise features you haven't built. "We're working on QuickBooks API integration in Phase 2" is honest. "It works with QuickBooks today" is a lie that will catch up to you.
- Don't slam Procore or Cantina by name. Stay above. "Procore is great if you're $50M+; we built for the band that's been overlooked."
- Don't end without a specific next-step ask.

## Yellow flags (probably not your customer right now)

- They use Procore happily. (You'll have to fight uphill.)
- They have an in-house developer. (They'll want to build, not buy.)
- They run multiple entities or international projects. (Out of MVP scope.)
- They tell you "we're fine with what we have." (Believe them. Move on.)

## Green flags (your ideal customer)

- They mention Excel multiple times unprompted.
- They mention "Sal's pain" — gap-stops, sub formats, chasing PMs.
- The Controller is on the call AND the Principal. (Both feel the pain; both have buy-in power.)
- They ask "when can I see this live with my data?" (Implicit yes.)
- They have a specific project they want to use it on next month.
