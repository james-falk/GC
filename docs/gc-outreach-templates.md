# Outreach Templates

For initial contact with mid-market commercial GCs. **Goal: get to a 30-min discovery call.** Not selling — getting permission to listen.

Three angles per channel. Pick the one that fits the prospect best. Personalize every send — generic outreach in construction has near-zero response.

---

## LinkedIn DMs

### A. The "research" angle (recommended default)

**Subject (if applicable):** Quick question about your pay-app cycle

> Hi [First Name] — I'm [James] · I'm building software for $5M–$50M commercial GCs and doing 10 conversations this month with firms in your size range to understand how the monthly pay-app cycle actually works in practice.
>
> Not pitching — I'm trying to learn. Specifically: how change orders propagate to your sub contracts and SoVs, and where the cycle stalls.
>
> 30 min on Zoom in the next two weeks?

Why it works: low ask (research, not sale), specific (pay-app cycle), peer-to-peer tone.

### B. The "specific project" angle (when you can reference real work)

> Hi [First Name] — saw your firm finished [specific project name from their LinkedIn or website] recently. Nice work on [specific detail — sustainability, on-time delivery, something concrete].
>
> Quick reason for the message: I'm building software for mid-market commercial GCs — specifically around the pay-app and change-order workflow that I've heard from a few firms is mostly Excel + email + chasing PMs. Curious whether that resonates with how Lincoln-style projects run for you.
>
> Open to a 30-min Zoom?

Why it works: shows you did your homework, anchors on a real shared reference, then pivots cleanly.

### C. The "pain probe" angle (more direct, higher risk/reward)

> Hi [First Name] — direct question: when a change order gets approved by the owner but doesn't get entered into your SoV tracker for a week or two, and a sub submits a pay app that exceeds their contract — does that happen often at your firm?
>
> If yes: I built something for that. 30 min, no pitch — I want to learn how you handle it today.
>
> If no: ignore me, you've got it figured out.

Why it works: instant qualifier. If they laugh and say "every month", you've got a hot lead. If silence, move on.

## Email templates

### A. The discovery-first email (recommended for cold)

**Subject:** Quick question about how [Company] runs pay-app cycles

> Hi [First Name],
>
> I'm James Falk, building software for $5M–$50M commercial general contractors. Doing 10 conversations this month with firms in your size band to understand how the monthly pay-app cycle actually runs.
>
> The pattern I keep hearing:
> - Schedule of values lives in Excel, change orders in Word templates, approvals in email, and the whole thing has to reconcile back to QuickBooks somehow.
> - When a CO gets approved but the SoV doesn't get updated in time, a sub's invoice slips, AP chases the PM, the cycle stalls.
> - Existing tools (Procore, Cantina, GCPay) cover slices but the data drift between them is the actual pain.
>
> Curious whether any of that sounds familiar at [Company].
>
> Not pitching — I'd love 30 min on Zoom in the next two weeks to learn how you run it. Happy to share what I've built only if it's relevant.
>
> James Falk · 734-299-0178 · jfalksolutions@gmail.com

Why it works: the second paragraph is the hook — they're nodding while they read it.

## Subject line bank (pick by gut)

- Quick question about [Company]'s pay-app cycle
- Built for $5–50M commercial GCs — would value your read
- 30 min — listening, not pitching
- Pay-app drift detection — research call
- Saw [recent project] — quick question on how it billed

## Send-time conventions

- Tuesday or Wednesday, 9:30am–11am local time. Highest open rates for B2B in construction.
- Avoid Mondays (catch-up day) and Fridays (mental check-out).
- One follow-up after 5–7 business days. Then move on. Two follow-ups is the maximum without a response — three becomes pestering.

## What to track per send

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Date sent | timing |
| Channel (LinkedIn / email) | conversion comparison |
| Template variant (A/B/C) | iteration |
| Response received? | response rate |
| Call booked? | conversion to call |
| Call held? | show rate |
| Outcome | qualified / not / referred |

Aim for: 30 sends → 5 responses → 3 calls booked → 2 calls held → 1 design-partner-style commitment. That ratio is realistic in construction; most B2B SaaS expects better but construction sales is slower.
